Pico W radio projects

PicoShack

PicoShack turns a Raspberry Pi Pico W into a compact radio automation toolkit. It controls a connected radio’s PTT and feeds audio through a radio interface, with two selectable operating features: PicoFox for an automatic fox-hunt transmitter and PicoCW for manually keyed Morse audio. Its own Wi-Fi access point puts configuration and operating controls on a phone or laptop, wherever the project is deployed.

PicoFox

An automatic fox transmitter

PicoFox repeats a sequence of Morse fox identifiers, a two-tone warble, a frequency sweep, and a station ID, with off-air pauses between stages. Set the callsign, fox identifier, tones, timing, and audio gain from the browser. The operating page shows the live sequence and provides Start/Stop controls and an optional browser audio monitor. Stopping sends a closing station ID and leaves Wi-Fi available.

DTMF remote control

Control the fox over the air

Conditioned receiver-speaker audio lets PicoShack listen for DTMF commands while PTT is off. Send *1 to start, *0 to stop with a station ID, *2 to identify, *3 to restart the sequence, or *4 to run one complete cycle. Commands *5, *6, and *7 play the fox identifier, warble, and sweep. A courtesy tone acknowledges accepted commands. The star prefix helps prevent accidental activation; it is not authentication.

PicoCW

MCW with a key, paddles, or browser

Modulated continuous wave (MCW) sends Morse as audio through the radio’s microphone input. PicoCW supports a straight key and iambic Mode A or B paddles, with adjustable speed, tone, paddle polarity, and PTT hang time. Use physical key inputs or the web page’s straight-key and DIT/DAH controls; the left and right arrow keys also operate the keyer from a keyboard.

Wi-Fi configuration

A local web page, no internet needed

Join the PicoShack Wi-Fi network, then open http://picoshack.local/ or http://192.168.4.1 in a browser. Dedicated PicoFox and PicoCW pages keep operating controls close at hand. Device settings select the power-up feature and change the access-point name and password. Applied settings are saved to flash and restored after power cycles; Wi-Fi changes take effect after reboot.
